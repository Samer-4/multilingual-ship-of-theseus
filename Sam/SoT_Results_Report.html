Ship of Theseus: rewriting and what survives

CS 6120 · Sam Wong · Ship of Theseus paraphrased corpus (Tripto et al.) · RQ1 decay, RQ2 source attribution, RQ3 rewriter fingerprint

When a text is rewritten three times in a row (T0 → T1 → T2 → T3), how much of its wording, meaning, style and source identity survives? And does English → Spanish → English round-trip translation behave differently from English paraphrasing (ChatGPT, PaLM, Dipper, Pegasus)?

RQ1 · decay
Meaning outlasts wording and style

Most of the loss happens in round 1 (71–90%). Style is lost faster than content for every paraphraser by T3.

RQ1 · translation
Translation preserves more on every metric

On the same 178 XSum documents: BERTScore 0.81 vs 0.58 at T3. Translation settles after one round; paraphrase keeps drifting.

RQ2 · attribution
Paraphrase erases more of the source

Translators keep 72–85% of the source signal, most paraphrasers 29–55%. ChatGPT relabels 55% of non-OpenAI texts as OpenAI.

RQ3 · fingerprint
Both families leave one, of different kinds

Paraphrasers are recognised by style and length, and their fingerprint grows each round. Translators are recognised by wording and characters.

Data and method

Corpus7 datasets (cmv, eli5, sci_gen, tldr, wp, xsum, yelp); 7 sources (Human + 6 LLMs)
Sample16,682 originals that every paraphraser completed to T3, on 2,996 articles; 583,870 pairs
Translation setSamer Ahmed's XSum round trips (MADLAD-400-3B, NLLB-200-1.3B, Qwen3-4B; 3 rounds). Matched set: 178 documents, 31 articles that every rewriter completed
Statistical unitthe original article. 95% CIs from an article-cluster bootstrap; classifier folds grouped by article
Retained scale1 = identical to T0, 0 = no closer than an unrelated T0 text from the same dataset (floor = mean of 200 unrelated T0 pairs per dataset). Similarities: (s − floor)/(1 − floor); distances: (floor − d)/floor
ValidationSamer's originals are byte-identical to ours; our code reproduces 11 of his per-pair metrics and 100% of his RQ2 T0 predictions

RQ2 · Can the original source still be identified?

A classifier trained only on originals (7 sources, chance macro-F1 0.143) is applied to rewrites. Samer's protocol, ported verbatim. The ~30-word opening shared by all sources is removed. Retained attribution = (F1 at Tk − chance)/(F1 at T0 − chance).

Much of what survives paraphrasing is length

Dipper's text models are no better than length alone at T3 (gain 0.00). With every text cut to 50 words, only Pegasus keeps a real signal (0.34 vs chance 0.14 on the full corpus; ChatGPT 0.16, PaLM 0.22, Dipper 0.19).

Pegasus shortens text to 67% with short sentences and few commas; Dipper shortens to 77%, lowers vocabulary diversity and adds curly quotes; ChatGPT trims to 85% with a stable, comma-rich style. Translators keep length at 92–99%, so only finer lexical and orthographic choices separate them. MADLAD's curly quotes are a large part of the translator signal.

Caveats

  • Six of the seven sources are LLM-generated, so a paraphraser may push a text toward a style it already had.
  • The translation comparison is small and narrow: XSum only, 31 articles, one language pair (Spanish), greedy decoding. MT models translate sentence by sentence, Qwen whole documents.
  • Word retention and style are not compared for translation, because the translated texts are not in the repo.
  • Information metrics are heuristics: a paraphrased entity name or reformatted number counts as a loss.
  • RQ2/RQ3 use fixed, untuned classical models: they measure relative survival of the signal, not the best achievable attribution.
  • Invalid generations are counted as attrition and excluded, never repaired.
